# Textures and patterns

Recipe for seamless textures, repeating patterns, and material swatches. Thinner doctrine than the other intents — extend it as real usage teaches more.

- Say **"seamless repeating pattern, tileable edges"** explicitly when tiling matters; nothing about tiling is implied by "texture" alone. Verify tiling by eye — the model approximates it, and edges may still need post-cleanup.
- Lead with the **material, not the scene**: _"brushed steel surface"_, _"hand-thrown ceramic glaze"_, _"woven linen weave"_. Concrete material and finish words carry the result: matte, glossy, grainy, brushed, woven, cracked, weathered, embossed.
- **Flat, even, diffuse lighting; no focal subject; uniform density edge to edge.** A texture with a highlight or a hero element tiles badly and reads as a photo, not a surface.
- **Top-down / straight-on view** unless a specific relief angle is wanted; add _"consistent scale across the surface"_ for pattern work.
- Anchor the palette with named colors or supplied hexes; one or two colors for pattern work, or the pattern drifts.
- Square sizes tile best; draft at `quality: low` — texture quality differences between tiers are smaller than for subjects, so promote only when detail genuinely matters.
- Constraint block: _"no text, no watermark, no focal object, no vignette, uniform lighting"_.
